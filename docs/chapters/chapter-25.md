# Chapter XXV — Evidence in Inquiries and Trials

## Sections 307–309 — The Court's own language, the accused's right to be present for evidence, and the summons-case memorandum

### The dispute this solves

::: oneminute Before any evidence can be recorded, two structural questions have to be settled: in what language does this Court function, and who must be present while evidence is being taken? Section 307 answers the first — the State Government fixes the language of every Court in the State other than the High Court. Section 308 answers the second with a strong default: evidence must be taken in the accused's presence, or, where his personal attendance is dispensed with, in his advocate's presence — and that presence can now be satisfied through audio-video electronic means at a State-Government-notified place, a deliberate accommodation for an accused who cannot or should not physically travel to a crowded courtroom. But the default bends the other way for one category of witness: a woman below eighteen alleged to have been raped or subjected to another sexual offence — there, the Court may take steps to ensure she is not confronted by the accused, while still preserving his right to cross-examine her. Section 309 then supplies the recording standard for the lightest-weight proceedings — summons-cases, the s.164-167 inquiries, and s.491 proceedings outside a trial — a memorandum of the substance of the evidence, not a verbatim transcript, made by the Magistrate himself or, if he genuinely cannot, dictated in open Court after he records why.
:::

::: story A courtroom, a screen, and a witness who never has to see the man she is testifying against
A trial is underway for an alleged sexual offence against a girl aged sixteen. The Magistrate, invoking section 308's proviso, arranges for her testimony to be given from a separate room, visible to the Court and to the accused's advocate over video, without her being made to sit across from the accused himself. The accused's advocate is still permitted to cross-examine her in full — nothing in this arrangement narrows that right — but the confrontation itself, which section 308 was amended to specifically guard against for this category of victim, never happens. Meanwhile, in an unrelated summons-case being heard the same week, the Magistrate records only a memorandum of the substance of each witness's evidence, in the language the State Government has fixed for that Court under section 307 — not a word-for-word transcript, since summons-cases do not carry that weight.
:::

> **307.** The State Government [[?may]] determine what shall be, for purposes of this Sanhita, [[!the language of each Court]] within the State other than the High Court.
>
> **308.** Except as otherwise expressly provided, all evidence taken in the course of the trial or other proceeding [[!shall]] be taken in the presence of the accused, or, when his personal attendance is dispensed with, in the presence of his advocate including through audio-video electronic means at the designated place to be notified by the State Government:
>
> Provided that where the evidence of a woman below the age of eighteen years who is alleged to have been subjected to rape or any other sexual offence, is to be recorded, the Court [[?may]] take appropriate measures to ensure that such woman is not confronted by the accused while at the same time ensuring the right of cross-examination of the accused.
>
> *Explanation.* — In this section, "accused" includes a person in relation to whom any proceeding under Chapter IX has been commenced under this Sanhita. ⚠️ *Chapter IX has not yet been built in this repository — likely the maintenance-of-dependants jurisdiction, given the roadmap's Phase 8 placement.*
>
> **309. (1)** In all summons-cases tried before a Magistrate, in all inquiries under sections 164 to 167 (both inclusive), and in all proceedings under section 491 otherwise than in the course of a trial, the Magistrate [[!shall]], as the examination of each witness proceeds, make a memorandum of the substance of the evidence in the language of the Court:
>
> Provided that if the Magistrate is unable to make such memorandum himself, he [[!shall]], after recording the reason of his inability, cause such memorandum to be made in writing or from his dictation in open Court.
>
> **(2)** Such memorandum [[!shall]] be signed by the Magistrate and [[!shall]] form part of the record.

::: proviso The audio-video accommodation and the child-witness protection are two separate, independently justified departures from the "in presence" default
Section 308's audio-video electronic means option is available generally, whenever personal attendance has been dispensed with, for the ordinary reason of practical accommodation. The proviso protecting a woman below eighteen alleged to be a victim of rape or another sexual offence is narrower and purpose-specific — it exists to prevent a particular, recognised harm (confrontation trauma), and it comes with its own built-in limit: the accused's cross-examination right survives intact. Neither departure dilutes the other.
:::

::: proviso Section 309's "memorandum of the substance," not a verbatim record, is deliberately lighter than the recording standards built in the next cluster
Summons-cases, the specific s.164-167 inquiries (⚠️ not yet independently verified in this repository), and out-of-trial s.491 proceedings (⚠️ not yet built) get a substance-only memorandum. The next cluster's ss.310-311 impose a materially heavier verbatim-recording standard for warrant-cases and Sessions trials — the difference in recording weight tracks the difference in the proceeding's own gravity, a pattern already familiar from this guide's earlier trial chapters.
:::

### In plain English

Three separate questions, three separate answers. What language does this Court even work in — fixed once, administratively, by the State Government. Who has to be in the room while evidence is taken — the accused himself by default, his advocate instead if his own attendance is excused, and either can now appear over video rather than in person. And is there a reason to depart even further from the ordinary presence rule — yes, for a specific, narrow category: a young rape or sexual-offence complainant, who can be shielded from actually confronting the accused without losing his right to cross-examine her. Then, once evidence actually starts being recorded, how much detail gets written down depends on how serious the proceeding is — summons-cases and a few specific out-of-trial proceedings get only the gist.

### What changed from the CrPC

⚠️ *Provisional mapping, pending dedicated verification pass: BNSS section 307 appears to track CrPC section 272 (language of Courts), BNSS section 308 appears to track CrPC section 273 (evidence to be taken in presence of accused), and BNSS section 309 appears to track CrPC section 274 (record in summons-cases and inquiries). The audio-video electronic means language in section 308, and the proviso protecting a woman below eighteen alleged to be a rape/sexual-offence victim from confrontation, both read as likely genuine BNSS-era additions — consistent with this guide's broader pattern of BNSS adding audio-video accommodations and victim-protective measures throughout the trial chapters — but neither addition is independently verified against a bare CrPC text held in this repository.*

### Test your instinct

:::: instinct In a trial where the accused's personal attendance has been dispensed with, his advocate is present in the courtroom throughout, but evidence is nonetheless recorded without notifying either the accused or his advocate in advance of an audio-video link, since the advocate was physically present anyway.
::: ruling No violation — physical presence of the advocate already satisfies section 308
Section 308 requires either the accused's presence or, where dispensed with, his advocate's presence — "including through audio-video electronic means" is an additional mode of satisfying that presence requirement, not a mandatory one. Physical presence of the advocate in the courtroom fully satisfies section 308 without any audio-video arrangement being necessary at all.
:::
::::

:::: instinct A Magistrate hearing a summons-case records only a brief memorandum of the substance of a witness's evidence, rather than a verbatim word-for-word account, and defence counsel objects that the record is incomplete.
::: ruling Section 309 does not require a verbatim record for a summons-case
Section 309(1) expressly calls for "a memorandum of the substance of the evidence," not a full transcript. The heavier verbatim standard applies to warrant-cases and Sessions trials under ss.310-311, not to summons-cases — the Magistrate's memorandum-only record is exactly what the section requires.
:::

#### Sword and shield

As a **shield** for a young sexual-offence complainant, section 308's proviso is a concrete tool counsel for the prosecution or the victim can invoke early — before the witness is called — to secure a non-confrontational recording arrangement, rather than waiting for distress to surface mid-examination. As a **sword** for the defence, the same proviso's own internal limit is worth stating on the record at the outset: whatever confrontation-avoidance measure is adopted, the right to cross-examine the witness must remain genuinely intact, and any arrangement that dilutes it in substance (not merely in form) is open to challenge.

#### The limitation clock

No filing deadline is fixed by ss.307-309; section 307's language determination is a standing administrative act, and the recording obligations of section 309 arise continuously as each witness is examined.

#### Strategy and drafting

**Model checklist for reviewing this cluster's provisions:**

1. Confirm the Court's fixed language under section 307 before assuming a translation or interpretation issue is even live — it may already be administratively settled.
2. In a case involving a young alleged rape or sexual-offence victim, raise section 308's proviso proactively, before the witness is called, rather than reactively once distress becomes apparent.
3. When challenging a confrontation-avoidance arrangement under section 308's proviso, frame the objection around whether cross-examination remains genuinely effective in substance, not merely whether the form used still allows questions to be put.
4. Do not object to a summons-case memorandum for lacking verbatim detail — section 309 does not promise a transcript, and the objection will not succeed on that ground alone.
