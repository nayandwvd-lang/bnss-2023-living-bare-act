# Glossary

Terms of art as they are met in the guide. Every entry below is grounded in the provision that actually defines or uses the term — nothing here is a general dictionary definition imported from outside the site. Entries are added as the provisions that define or depend on them are written.

| Term | Meaning | Where it is met |
|---|---|---|
| Cognizable offence | An offence for which a police officer may arrest without a warrant and investigate without the Magistrate's prior authorisation. The classification (cognizable / non-cognizable) is fixed in advance by the First Schedule, not decided case by case. | [Chapter XIII](/chapters/chapter-13) — s. 173, information in cognizable cases |
| Non-cognizable offence | An offence for which a police officer may neither arrest without a warrant nor investigate without an order from a Magistrate. The officer's duty on receiving such information is limited to entering it in a register and directing the informant to the Magistrate. | [Chapter XIII](/chapters/chapter-13) — ss. 174–175, non-cognizable information |
| Summons-case | A case relating to an offence not being a warrant-case — in practice, the lighter procedural track under which the Magistrate issues a summons rather than a warrant to secure the accused's attendance. | [Chapter XVII](/chapters/chapter-17) — ss. 227–229, issue of process |
| Warrant-case | A case relating to an offence punishable with death, imprisonment for life, or imprisonment exceeding two years — the heavier procedural track, under which the Magistrate may issue a warrant (or, if he thinks fit, a summons) in the first instance. | [Chapter XVII](/chapters/chapter-17) — ss. 227–229, issue of process |
| Petty offence | An offence that a Magistrate taking cognizance may dispose of summarily under section 283 or section 284, triggering the postal guilty-plea shortcut — the accused may plead guilty and pay a fine by post without appearing in person. | [Chapter XVII](/chapters/chapter-17) — s. 229; [Chapter XXII](/chapters/chapter-22) — ss. 283–288, summary trials |
| Complaint case | A criminal case instituted otherwise than on a police report — i.e., begun by a private complaint made directly to a Magistrate, as distinct from a case that reaches the Magistrate through a police investigation and report. | [Chapter XVII](/chapters/chapter-17) — ss. 232–233, committal and the parallel-investigation stay |
| Indigent person | A person unable to furnish a bail bond. The Explanation to section 478 supplies a working presumption: failure to arrange a bail bond within a week of arrest is, by itself, sufficient ground to presume indigence — the person does not have to separately prove poverty. | [Chapter XXXV](/chapters/chapter-35) — s. 478, bail as of right in bailable offences |
| "Just and proper" | The residual discretionary standard under section 480's second proviso, letting a Court release on bail a person otherwise excluded under section 480(1)(ii) (repeat cognizable offenders), for "any other special reason" it finds just and proper. It is a safety valve, not a fixed test — the site's own s. 480 page flags it as the clause practitioners litigate most, precisely because "reasonable grounds for believing" and "just and proper" both carry real weight without a mechanical formula. | [Chapter XXXV](/chapters/chapter-35) — s. 480, bail in non-bailable offences |
| Bailable warrant | A warrant of arrest that carries a bail direction already built in — issued under section 482(3) once a court has granted an anticipatory-bail direction under section 482(1)–(2), so that if a Magistrate later decides a warrant (rather than a summons) should issue in the first instance, that warrant is automatically a bailable one conforming to the court's earlier direction. | [Chapter XXXV](/chapters/chapter-35) — s. 482, anticipatory bail |
| Six months | The default duration for which a bond to appear before the Appellate Court, executed under section 481 before conclusion of trial or disposal of appeal, remains in force. | [Chapter XXXV](/chapters/chapter-35) — s. 481, bond to appear before the Appellate Court |
| Preventive detention | Detention under any law providing specifically for preventive detention (as opposed to ordinary custody following arrest on an accusation) — a person so detained still falls within this Chapter's definition of a person "detained" whose attendance in court can be compelled. | [Chapter XXIV](/chapters/chapter-24) — ss. 301–303, attendance of persons confined or detained |
| Examination-in-chief | The first stage of a witness's oral evidence, led by the party who called the witness (as governed by BSA 2023 s. 142). Section 183(6)(b) treats certain Magistrate-recorded statements of a disabled victim/witness as standing in place of a fresh examination-in-chief at trial, so the maker can be cross-examined on the recorded statement without re-examination in court. | [Chapter XIII](/chapters/chapter-13) — s. 183, recording of confessions and statements |
| "May amount to contradiction" | The standard, from the Explanation to the section 180 statement-to-police provisions, for when an *omission* (not just a positive inconsistency) in a witness's police statement can be used to contradict that witness at trial — only where the omission is significant and relevant in context; whether it actually amounts to a contradiction is a question of fact for the Court. | [Chapter XIII](/chapters/chapter-13) — ss. 179–182, statements to police during investigation |
| "Relative" | For the purposes of the mandatory-inquiry provisions on custodial or dowry-related death, defined to mean parents, children, brothers, sisters and spouse — a closed, specific list rather than the word's ordinary broader sense. | [Chapter XIII](/chapters/chapter-13) — ss. 194–196, inquest into unnatural death |
| Section 51 (cross-reference) | The provision (Chapter V, medical examination of the accused) that supplies the working meanings of "examination" and "registered medical practitioner" wherever those terms recur elsewhere in the Sanhita, including in the medical-examination-of-a-rape-victim provision. | [Chapter V](/chapters/chapter-05) — ss. 51–53, medical examination |
| Section 58 (cross-reference) | The provision fixing the twenty-four-hour clock within which an arrested person must be produced before a Magistrate — the reference point that section 187's "cannot be completed within twenty-four hours" test is measured against. | [Chapter V](/chapters/chapter-05) — ss. 55–59, the twenty-four-hour production clock |
| Section 103 (cross-reference) | The general provision governing how every search-warrant under this Chapter is actually executed — the witnessing, list-making and (new in the BNSS) video-recording requirements that a police-station search under section 185–186 also draws on by cross-reference. | [Chapter VII](/chapters/chapter-07) — ss. 102–105, execution of search-warrants |
