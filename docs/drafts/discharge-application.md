# Model discharge application

::: oneminute When to use this
An application under **section 250 of the BNSS** — the accused's structured, time-bound opportunity to end a Sessions-triable case before trial, once it has been committed to the Court of Session under section 232. This is not an acquittal application and not a quashing petition (that is [sections 527–528](/drafts/quashing-petition), the inherent-powers route, available at any stage and in any court). Use this only after committal, and only within the window the statute itself sets — miss it, and this specific route closes.
:::

## Eligibility checklist, before you draft

::: proviso Confirm these before investing in the application
1. Confirm the case has actually been committed to the Court of Session under section 232 — section 250(1)'s sixty-day clock runs from the date of commitment, not from arrest, chargesheet, or any earlier event.
2. Calendar the sixty-day window from commitment immediately on taking instructions. Nothing in sections 250–251 provides for a late application on the ground of newly discovered material — that argument, if made at all, belongs at the section 251 charge-framing stage, not as a late discharge application.
3. Draft against the correct standard: grave suspicion sufficient to form a presumptive opinion the accused committed the offence — not proof beyond reasonable doubt, and not the prosecution's bare say-so either.
4. Have a fallback ready. If discharge fails, section 251 asks whether the offence is exclusively triable by the Court of Session — if it is not, the case is transferred down to a Magistrate as a warrant case, a live and separate point worth flagging in argument even when discharge itself is refused.
:::

```

IN THE MATTER OF:
SESSIONS CASE NO. _____ OF [YEAR]
(Committed vide order dated [DATE] under section 232 of the BNSS)
FIR/CASE NO. _____ OF [YEAR], POLICE STATION: [NAME]
UNDER SECTION(S): [BNS SECTION(S) — verify against the charge sheet, do not
assume]

[NAME OF ACCUSED]
S/o / D/o / W/o [NAME], aged about [AGE] years,
R/o [ADDRESS]                                                    ... ACCUSED

VERSUS

STATE OF [STATE]                                                  ... RESPONDENT

APPLICATION FOR DISCHARGE UNDER SECTION 250 OF THE
BHARATIYA NAGARIK SURAKSHA SANHITA, 2023

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

1.  That the above-noted case was committed to this Hon'ble Court by order
    dated [DATE] under section 232 of the BNSS, and this application is
    filed within sixty days of that date, as required by section 250(1).

2.  That the record of the case and the documents submitted therewith,
    considered as a whole, do not disclose sufficient ground for proceeding
    against the accused, for the following reasons:

    (a) [STATE THE SPECIFIC EVIDENTIARY GAP — e.g., absence of material
        connecting the accused to the alleged act].

    (b) [STATE ANY MATERIAL CONTRADICTION between the prosecution's own
        witnesses or documents].

    (c) [STATE ANY LEGAL INFIRMITY — e.g., the material, even taken at its
        highest, does not disclose the ingredients of the offence
        alleged].

3.  That the material on record does not rise to the level of grave
    suspicion sufficient to form a presumptive opinion that the accused
    committed the offence alleged, as required for proceeding to charge —
    it being submitted that the Court is not a mere post office for the
    prosecution's material, nor is the accused required to disprove the
    charge at this stage.

4.  That the accused has fully cooperated with the investigation and
    reserves the right to raise, in the alternative and without prejudice
    to this application, the question of whether the offence(s) alleged
    are exclusively triable by this Hon'ble Court, should this application
    not succeed.

PRAYER

It is, therefore, most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be
pleased to:

(a) discharge the accused in the above-noted case under section 250(2) of
    the BNSS, recording reasons therefor; and

(b) pass such other order(s) as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper
    in the interest of justice.

AND for this act of kindness, the accused shall, as duty bound, ever pray.

[PLACE]
[DATE]
                                                          ACCUSED
                                                          Through Counsel
                                                          [ADVOCATE NAME]
                                                          [ENROLMENT NO.]
```

## Drafting pitfalls

::: warning Before you file
1. Do not draft paragraph 2 as a generic denial. Target the specific evidentiary gaps or contradictions on the record as it stands — a discharge application that merely asserts innocence, without engaging the actual material, does not meet the grave-suspicion standard the Judge is applying.
2. Do not argue the *Prafulla Kumar Samal* standard as though it required proof beyond reasonable doubt at this stage — that over-argues the point and invites the Court to treat the application as premature. Equally, do not concede that any suspicion at all suffices — that under-argues it.
3. Do not let the section 251 classification question go unraised as a fallback. If the offence alleged may not, in fact, be exclusively triable by the Court of Session, flag it — a successful transfer down under section 251(1)(a) moves the case to the comparatively faster warrant-case track before a Magistrate, even where discharge itself fails.
4. Do not confuse this application's sixty-day clock (from commitment) with the separate sixty-day clock the Judge works to under section 251(1)(b) for framing the charge (from the first hearing on charge) — they run from different trigger dates and neither substitutes for the other.
:::

::: danger The threshold trap
Section 250(1) frames the sixty-day window as when the accused "may prefer" a discharge application — and nothing in the text authorises a late application, including on the ground of newly discovered exculpatory material. An application filed after the window has lapsed risks being treated as not maintainable under section 250 at all, whatever its merits; new material discovered late is better raised at the section 251 charge-framing stage than gambled on a late discharge application.
:::

## Timing

Calendar the sixty-day window from the date of commitment the moment instructions are taken in a committed matter — this is the accused's own responsibility to meet, and section 250(2)'s discharge power is framed as a response to an application made within that window, not an independent power the Judge exercises on his own motion. If discharge fails or is not sought, the separate sixty-day charge-framing clock under section 251(1)(b) becomes the next calendar date to track.
