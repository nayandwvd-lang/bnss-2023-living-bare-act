# Model bail application (non-bailable offence)

::: oneminute When to use this
An application under **section 480 of the BNSS** — the route to bail for a person accused of a non-bailable offence, arrested or detained without warrant, or appearing before a Court other than the High Court or Court of Session. This is not the bailable-offence route (that is [section 478](/chapters/chapter-35), a matter of right, not application) and it is not the pre-arrest route (that is [anticipatory bail under section 482](/drafts/anticipatory-bail-application)). Use this once the person is already in custody or facing a warrant on a non-bailable accusation.
:::

## Eligibility checklist, before you draft

::: proviso Confirm these before investing in the application
1. The offence is genuinely non-bailable — check the classification against the First Schedule, not against how serious the offence "feels."
2. Screen for the two section 480(1) exclusions: reasonable grounds of guilt on a death-or-life-imprisonment offence (clause (i)), and — for a cognizable offence — a qualifying history of prior convictions (clause (ii)).
3. If an exclusion is engaged, identify which override applies on the facts: the child/woman/sick-or-infirm proviso, or the "just and proper... special reason" proviso.
4. If the applicant is in a seven-year-or-more offence, or an offence under the BNS chapters section 480(3) names, build the mandatory conditions into the draft order sought — do not leave them to the Court to improvise.
:::

## The model draft

```
IN THE COURT OF THE [MAGISTRATE / SESSIONS JUDGE], AT [PLACE]

BAIL APPLICATION NO. _____ OF [YEAR]

IN THE MATTER OF:
FIR/CASE NO. _____ OF [YEAR]
POLICE STATION: [NAME]
UNDER SECTION(S): [BNS SECTION(S) — verify against the charge sheet / FIR, do not assume]

[NAME OF APPLICANT]
S/o / D/o / W/o [NAME], aged about [AGE] years,
R/o [ADDRESS]                                                    ... APPLICANT / ACCUSED

VERSUS

STATE OF [STATE]
(through the Station House Officer, P.S. [NAME])                 ... RESPONDENT

APPLICATION FOR BAIL UNDER SECTION 480 OF THE
BHARATIYA NAGARIK SURAKSHA SANHITA, 2023

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

1.  That the applicant is the accused in the above-noted case registered at
    P.S. [NAME] under section(s) [BNS SECTION(S)], arrested on [DATE] and
    currently in [judicial / police] custody.

2.  That the facts and circumstances leading to the applicant's arrest are,
    briefly, as follows: [STATE FACTS CONCISELY AND CHRONOLOGICALLY —
    dates, times, and the applicant's own account of the incident].

3.  That the applicant does NOT fall within the exclusion in section
    480(1)(i), in that there are no reasonable grounds for believing the
    applicant guilty of an offence punishable with death or imprisonment
    for life, because [STATE WHY — absence of material, contradiction in
    the prosecution's own version, etc.].

    [OR, where clause (i) or (ii) is engaged: That although the applicant's
    case falls within section 480(1)(i)/(ii), the applicant is entitled to
    the benefit of the [first / second] proviso to section 480(1), being a
    [child / woman / person who is sick or infirm], OR because it is just
    and proper to release the applicant for the following special reason:
    STATE THE REASON.]

4.  That the applicant has no previous criminal antecedents involving a
    conviction of the kind described in section 480(1)(ii). [OR: state and
    distinguish any prior conviction.]

5.  That the investigation qua the applicant, including seizure of
    documents and recording of relevant statements, stands substantially
    complete, and the applicant's continued custody serves no further
    investigative purpose.

6.  That the applicant is a permanent resident of the above address, has
    strong roots in the community, and is not a flight risk.

7.  That the applicant undertakes to abide by all conditions this Hon'ble
    Court may impose, including under section 480(3) if applicable, and to
    cooperate fully with the investigation and trial.

8.  That the applicant is prepared to furnish bail bonds and sureties to
    the satisfaction of this Hon'ble Court.

9.  That this application is bona fide and is not filed to delay or defeat
    the ends of justice.

PRAYER

It is, therefore, most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be
pleased to:

(a) admit the applicant to bail in the above-noted case, on such terms and
    conditions as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit; and

(b) pass such other order(s) as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper
    in the interest of justice.

AND for this act of kindness, the applicant shall, as duty bound, ever
pray.

[PLACE]
[DATE]
                                                          APPLICANT
                                                          Through Counsel
                                                          [ADVOCATE NAME]
                                                          [ENROLMENT NO.]
```

## Drafting pitfalls

::: warning Before you file
1. Do not draft paragraph 3 generically — plead affirmatively against whichever of clause (i) or (ii) the prosecution is likely to invoke, on the specific material in the FIR/charge sheet, not in the abstract.
2. Where section 480(3)'s mandatory conditions apply (seven-year-or-more offence, or the named BNS chapters), draft the prayer to anticipate them rather than waiting for the Court to impose an unfavourable version.
3. Do not omit the third proviso to section 480(1) where relevant — a refusal grounded only on "possible identification" or "possible further custody" is not, by itself, a sufficient ground, and the application should say so by name if the State raises it.
:::

::: danger The threshold trap
If the offence is genuinely one carrying death or life imprisonment and the material genuinely discloses reasonable grounds of guilt, section 480(1)(i) is not a technicality to argue around — it is the statute's own threshold. An application that does not squarely address it, and instead argues only the merits of the larger case, invites a refusal that never reaches those merits at all.
:::

## Timing

There is no independent limitation period for filing a section 480 application — it may be made as soon as the person is in custody or facing a warrant. Where the case is triable exclusively by the Court of Session and moves to that stage, the separate sixty-day discharge clock under [section 250](/drafts/discharge-application) becomes the next calendar date to track, not a substitute for this application.
