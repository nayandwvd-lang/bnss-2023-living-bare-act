# Model anticipatory bail application

::: oneminute When to use this
An application under **section 482 of the BNSS** — the pre-arrest route, available only to the High Court or the Court of Session, for a person who has reason to believe he may be arrested on a non-bailable accusation and wants protection lined up before that arrest happens. This is not the post-arrest route (that is [section 480](/drafts/bail-application), for a person already in custody or facing a warrant). Use this only before arrest, and only once genuine apprehension of arrest exists — not as a speculative, generalised shield against any future accusation.
:::

## Eligibility checklist, before you draft

::: proviso Confirm these before investing in the application
1. Confirm the accusation does **not** fall within BNS 2023 section 65 or section 70(2) — section 482(4) removes this remedy entirely for those two categories, with no discretion to weigh sympathetic facts against the bar. ⚠️ *This site has not independently verified the content of BNS 2023 ss. 65 and 70(2) — confirm the actual offence classification against the FIR/complaint before relying on this exclusion either way.*
2. Confirm the application goes to the High Court or the Court of Session only — no Magistrate and no police officer has any part in granting this direction.
3. Confirm genuine, present apprehension of arrest exists on a specific accusation — this is not a general amnesty applied for in the abstract, and the Court's discretion under section 482(1) is exercised on the facts of the particular case.
4. Do not build the application around a checklist of self-imposed preconditions (surrendering a passport, furnishing character certificates, undertaking not to seek regular bail) — section 482(2) conditions attach to a grant; they are not gatekeeping requirements to the merits inquiry.
:::

## The model draft

```
IN THE COURT OF THE [SESSIONS JUDGE / HIGH COURT OF ___], AT [PLACE]

ANTICIPATORY BAIL APPLICATION NO. _____ OF [YEAR]

IN THE MATTER OF:
FIR/COMPLAINT NO. _____ OF [YEAR] (if registered; else state the accusation
and its source)
POLICE STATION: [NAME]
UNDER SECTION(S): [BNS SECTION(S) — verify against the FIR/complaint, do not
assume; if the accusation may fall under BNS s. 65 or s. 70(2), resolve this
BEFORE drafting further — section 482(4) bars this application outright for
those offences]

[NAME OF APPLICANT]
S/o / D/o / W/o [NAME], aged about [AGE] years,
R/o [ADDRESS]                                                    ... APPLICANT

VERSUS

STATE OF [STATE]
(through the Station House Officer, P.S. [NAME])                 ... RESPONDENT

APPLICATION FOR A DIRECTION UNDER SECTION 482 OF THE
BHARATIYA NAGARIK SURAKSHA SANHITA, 2023

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

1.  That the applicant has reason to believe that he may be arrested on an
    accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence, namely
    [DESCRIBE THE ACCUSATION AND ITS SOURCE — FIR, complaint, or
    credible information of an impending FIR], under section(s)
    [BNS SECTION(S)].

2.  That the accusation does not fall within section 65 or sub-section (2)
    of section 70 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and this application
    is accordingly maintainable under section 482 of the BNSS. [If there is
    any ambiguity on classification, address it affirmatively here rather
    than leaving it to be raised by the State.]

3.  That the facts and circumstances giving rise to the apprehension of
    arrest are, briefly, as follows: [STATE FACTS CONCISELY AND
    CHRONOLOGICALLY — the applicant's own account, why arrest is
    apprehended, and the basis for that belief].

4.  That the applicant has committed no offence and is being falsely
    implicated because [STATE WHY, ON THE SPECIFIC MATERIAL AVAILABLE —
    absence of material, mala fides, contradiction in the accusation's own
    version, etc.].

5.  That the applicant is a permanent resident of the above address, has
    strong roots in the community, and is not a flight risk.

6.  That the applicant is willing to abide by such conditions as this
    Hon'ble Court may deem fit to impose under section 482(2), including
    making himself available for interrogation as and when required, not
    tampering with evidence or influencing witnesses, and not leaving
    [India / the State] without the prior permission of this Hon'ble Court.

7.  That the applicant has no previous criminal antecedents. [OR: state and
    distinguish any prior involvement.]

8.  That this application is bona fide and is not filed to delay or defeat
    the ends of justice, or to pre-empt a legitimate investigation.

PRAYER

It is, therefore, most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be
pleased to:

(a) direct that in the event of the applicant's arrest in connection with
    the above accusation, he shall be released on bail, on such conditions
    as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit to impose under section 482(2) of the
    BNSS; and

(b) pass such other order(s) as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper
    in the interest of justice.

AND for this act of kindness, the applicant shall, as duty bound, ever
pray.

[PLACE]
[DATE]
                                                          APPLICANT
                                                          Through Counsel
                                                          [ADVOCATE NAME]
                                                          [ENROLMENT NO.]
```

## Drafting pitfalls

::: warning Before you file
1. Do not draft this application as though it must satisfy a rigid, informally-imposed checklist of pre-conditions — ground the argument in the specific facts of the case, per *Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia*. A Court cannot lawfully refuse to even entertain the application for want of a passport surrender or a character certificate; those are, at most, conditions on a grant, not gates to the merits.
2. Do not overlook section 482(4) at the intake stage. Confirm the accusation does not fall within BNS 2023 section 65 or section 70(2) before investing in an application the section itself bars — this is a threshold, not an argument to be made around.
3. Do not treat the conditions available under sub-section (2) as optional extras to be negotiated away once granted — they bind exactly as the ordinary section 480(3) conditions would, and the model draft's paragraph 6 should anticipate the conditions realistically likely to be imposed rather than leaving the Court to improvise them.
4. Do not assume sub-section (3)'s bailable-warrant consequence operates automatically on arrest — ensure the arresting officer and any Magistrate issuing a warrant are put on express notice of the section 482(1) direction, or the consequence has nothing to attach to.
:::

::: danger The threshold trap
If the accusation genuinely falls within BNS 2023 section 65 or section 70(2), section 482(4) removes this remedy from the table entirely — there is no discretion left to argue, however sympathetic the personal facts. An application that argues the merits without first confirming the offence classification invites a refusal that never reaches those merits at all.
:::

## Timing

There is no independent limitation period for filing a section 482 application — but timing here is substantive, not procedural. File the moment genuine apprehension of arrest crystallises. Waiting until arrest is imminent narrows the practical window for the Court to act; waiting until after arrest has already occurred defeats the purpose of the remedy altogether, since the person is then in the different, less favourable posture of [ordinary post-arrest bail under section 480](/drafts/bail-application).
