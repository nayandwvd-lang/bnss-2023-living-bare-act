# Model quashing petition (inherent powers)

::: oneminute When to use this
A petition invoking **section 528 of the BNSS** — the High Court's preserved inherent power to make such orders as may be necessary to give effect to any order under the Sanhita, to prevent abuse of the process of any Court, or otherwise to secure the ends of justice. This is a residual, safety-valve remedy, not a first resort — it is available only where no specific provision of the Sanhita already addresses the situation, or where the specific machinery available is genuinely inadequate. Do not use this as a faster shortcut to a remedy the Sanhita already provides by name; the High Court will ordinarily send the petitioner back to that specific provision.
:::

## Eligibility checklist, before you draft

::: proviso Confirm these before investing in the petition
1. Identify, and rule out, every specific provision of the Sanhita that might already address the situation. Section 528 is available only where those provisions do not adequately cover the case — a petition that does not affirmatively address this invites the response that a specific remedy already exists.
2. Confirm the relief sought falls within one of section 528's three named purposes: giving effect to an existing order under the Sanhita, preventing abuse of the process of any court, or otherwise securing the ends of justice. A petition that fits none of these is not a section 528 petition.
3. Confirm this is filed in the **High Court** — section 528 saves the inherent powers of the High Court specifically, not of a Magistrate or Sessions Court.
4. Have the underlying proceeding's record ready to show precisely how continuing it, or continuing without the order sought, amounts to an abuse of process or a miscarriage of justice on the facts — a bare assertion of inherent jurisdiction, without this showing, is the weakest form of this petition.
:::

## The model draft

```
IN THE HIGH COURT OF [STATE] AT [PLACE]

CRIMINAL MISC. PETITION (QUASHING) NO. _____ OF [YEAR]
[Invoking the inherent powers of this Hon'ble Court under section 528
of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023]

IN THE MATTER OF:
FIR/CASE NO. _____ OF [YEAR]
POLICE STATION: [NAME]
UNDER SECTION(S): [BNS SECTION(S) — verify against the FIR/charge sheet,
do not assume]
PENDING BEFORE: [COURT/MAGISTRATE, PLACE]

[NAME OF PETITIONER]
S/o / D/o / W/o [NAME], aged about [AGE] years,
R/o [ADDRESS]                                                  ... PETITIONER

VERSUS

STATE OF [STATE]
(through the Station House Officer, P.S. [NAME])
[AND, where applicable: NAME OF COMPLAINANT/RESPONDENT NO. 2]      ... RESPONDENT(S)

PETITION UNDER SECTION 528 OF THE
BHARATIYA NAGARIK SURAKSHA SANHITA, 2023
FOR QUASHING OF [FIR NO. ___ / PROCEEDINGS / ORDER DATED ___]

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

1.  That the petitioner is aggrieved by [DESCRIBE THE FIR / PROCEEDING /
    ORDER sought to be quashed, with date and forum].

2.  That the facts and circumstances giving rise to this petition are,
    briefly, as follows: [STATE FACTS CONCISELY AND CHRONOLOGICALLY].

3.  That continuation of the above proceedings amounts to an abuse of the
    process of the Court, because [STATE THE SPECIFIC GROUND — e.g., the
    allegations, even taken at their highest, do not disclose the
    ingredients of the offence alleged; the proceedings are mala fide and
    instituted with an ulterior motive; the dispute is essentially civil in
    nature dressed up as criminal; the matter has been compromised between
    the parties in a manner the law permits; or such other specific
    ground].

4.  That no specific provision of the Sanhita provides an adequate remedy
    for the grievance stated above, in that [STATE AFFIRMATIVELY WHY THE
    SPECIFIC MACHINERY OF THE SANHITA — revision, discharge, or otherwise —
    does not suffice on these facts]. This averment is made because
    section 528 is a residual power, and this Hon'ble Court is entitled to
    know why the petitioner has not pursued, or could not adequately be
    served by, a specific remedy.

5.  That interference by this Hon'ble Court under section 528 is necessary
    to secure the ends of justice / to prevent abuse of the process of the
    [COURT NAME] / to give effect to [SPECIFY ORDER, if applicable].

6.  That this petition is bona fide and is not filed to delay or defeat the
    ends of justice, or to shield the petitioner from a proceeding that
    ought properly to continue.

PRAYER

It is, therefore, most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be
pleased to:

(a) quash [THE FIR NO. ___ / THE PROCEEDINGS IN ___ / THE ORDER DATED ___]
    in exercise of its inherent powers under section 528 of the BNSS; and

(b) pass such other order(s) as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper
    in the interest of justice.

AND for this act of kindness, the petitioner shall, as duty bound, ever
pray.

[PLACE]
[DATE]
                                                          PETITIONER
                                                          Through Counsel
                                                          [ADVOCATE NAME]
                                                          [ENROLMENT NO.]
```

## Drafting pitfalls

::: warning Before you file
1. Do not file this petition as a first resort. Rule out, on the record, every specific provision of the Sanhita that might already cover the grievance — a discharge application under [section 250](/drafts/discharge-application), a revision under [sections 438–442](/drafts/revision-application), or an appeal, as the case may be — and say so affirmatively in paragraph 4, rather than leaving the Court to work it out.
2. Do not use section 528 as a shortcut around a specific remedy merely because that remedy would take procedurally longer. The High Court is expected to send the petitioner back to the available specific machinery where one genuinely exists.
3. Do not plead paragraph 3 in the abstract. "Abuse of process" and "ends of justice" are not talismanic phrases — ground the argument in the specific material on record: what the FIR or complaint actually alleges, what it does not, and why continuing the proceeding on that material is unjust.
4. Do not overstate the citation position. No promoted Supreme Court or High Court authority is cited here for sections 527–528 specifically, given the recency of the renumbering — cite CrPC-era inherent-powers jurisprudence, if at all, only after independently re-verifying it applies to the substance of this provision, not by assuming continuity from memory.
:::

::: danger The threshold trap
Section 528 is powerful precisely because it is undefined — but that same open texture means a petition that does not show why the Sanhita's specific machinery is inadequate risks being read as an attempt to bypass provisions that already speak to the situation, rather than as a genuine invocation of a residual power. A petition that argues only the merits of the underlying dispute, without addressing the availability (or unavailability) of a specific remedy, invites a refusal on threshold grounds alone.
:::

## Timing

There is no independent limitation period for a section 528 petition — but it should ordinarily be made as promptly as the underlying need for intervention arises. Where the underlying proceeding carries its own clock — for instance, the [sixty-day discharge window under section 250](/drafts/discharge-application) — that clock is not paused or extended by an intention to file this petition; the two remedies should be assessed and, if necessary, pursued on their own separate timelines.
