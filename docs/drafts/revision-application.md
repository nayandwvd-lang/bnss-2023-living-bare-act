# Model revision application

::: oneminute When to use this
An application invoking **sections 438 and 442 of the BNSS** — the after-the-fact check on an inferior criminal court's correctness, legality, propriety, and regularity, filed either before a Sessions Judge (section 438) or the High Court (section 442). This is not an appeal, and it is not a fresh trial — it is a review of the record, bounded by hard guardrails: no interlocutory orders, one forum only, no converting an acquittal into a conviction, and ordinarily no revision where an appeal lay and was not brought. Use this where the finding, sentence, or order under challenge is not interlocutory, and where either no appeal lies at all, or an appeal was not brought under a genuine, explicable mistaken belief that none lay.
:::

## Eligibility checklist, before you draft

::: proviso Confirm these before choosing your forum
1. Confirm the order under challenge is **not interlocutory** — section 438(2) bars revisional power over any interlocutory order in an appeal, inquiry, trial, or other proceeding, outright.
2. Choose the forum — High Court or Sessions Judge — deliberately and finally. Section 438(3) is a strict one-forum rule: once an application has gone to either, no further application by the same person on the same matter will be entertained by the other, regardless of whether the first forum granted or refused relief.
3. If an appeal lay against the order and was not brought, section 442(4) ordinarily bars revision at the instance of the party who could have appealed. The only way around this is section 442(5)'s erroneous-belief exception — and that requires an affirmative, factual showing that the applicant genuinely believed no appeal lay, plus a separate showing that treating the revision as an appeal is necessary in the interests of justice. Do not assume the Court will infer either element.
4. If the relief sought would convert an acquittal into a conviction, stop — section 442(3) is an absolute bar with no exception. That relief, if available at all, lies only in a section 419 appeal against acquittal, not in revision.
5. If co-convicts from the same trial have split their applications between the High Court and a Sessions Judge, raise the section 443 forum-allocation question with the High Court promptly, rather than letting both proceedings run independently.
:::

    Court's attention to section 443 for its consideration on forum

(a) call for and examine the record of the proceedings in [CASE NUMBER]

(b) set aside / modify the order dated [DATE] to the extent stated above;

(c) pending disposal, suspend execution of the order/sentence under
    challenge and release the applicant on bond/bail, if in confinement;
    and

(d) pass such other order(s) as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper
    in the interest of justice.

pray.

## Drafting pitfalls

::: warning Before you file
1. Screen the order under challenge for its interlocutory character before filing. An application against a genuinely interlocutory order is barred outright by section 438(2) and invites summary rejection — do not argue around this, resolve it before drafting.
2. Do not file in both forums, and do not hedge by filing in one intending to try the other later. Section 438(3)'s one-forum rule is absolute and applies regardless of the outcome at the first forum.
3. Always check, before filing, whether an appeal lay and was not brought. If so, section 442(4) is a clean, threshold bar the opposing side will raise immediately — plead the section 442(5) erroneous-belief exception explicitly, with supporting facts, rather than leaving the point to be inferred.
4. Never frame a prayer that would, in substance, convert an acquittal into a conviction. Section 442(3) forecloses this entirely and without exception — that relief belongs in a section 419 appeal against acquittal, not here.
5. Where seeking a further-inquiry direction under section 439 against a discharged person, build the show-cause opportunity into the application itself — propose that the discharged person be put on notice and heard, rather than waiting for the court to raise the proviso on its own; a direction issued without that opportunity is vulnerable to challenge on that ground alone.
:::

::: danger The threshold trap
Revision is not a second appeal by another name. A party who had an appeal available and simply did not use it will ordinarily be turned away under section 442(4) — the erroneous-belief exception in section 442(5) is narrow, requires satisfying the Court on two separate elements, and is not an automatic escape hatch. An application that argues only the merits, without first clearing this threshold, invites a refusal that never reaches those merits.
:::

## Timing

Neither cluster prescribes an independent limitation period for filing a revision application — check the general limitation framework governing revision applications under this Sanhita rather than assuming one. Where section 442(5)'s erroneous-belief exception is invoked, the timing and explanation offered for the original mistaken filing bears directly on whether the interests-of-justice requirement is satisfied — delay in raising it, or a thin explanation, weakens that showing.
