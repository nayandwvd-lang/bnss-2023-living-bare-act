# Model maintenance application

::: oneminute When to use this
An application under **section 144 of the BNSS** — the summary, welfare-driven route for a wife, child, or parent who cannot maintain themselves to secure a monthly maintenance allowance from a person with sufficient means, without waiting out a full civil suit. This draft also covers the interim-maintenance route under section 144(1)'s second proviso, and points to the separate mechanisms for later alteration ([section 146](#cross-references)) and enforcement in another district ([section 147](#cross-references)). Use this where the dependant falls within one of section 144(1)'s four categories and the person against whom relief is sought has sufficient means but has neglected or refused to maintain them.
:::

## Eligibility checklist, before you draft

::: proviso Confirm these before drafting
1. Identify precisely which of the four section 144(1) categories the dependant falls into — a wife unable to maintain herself; a minor legitimate or illegitimate child; an adult child unable to maintain itself by reason of physical or mental abnormality or injury; or a father or mother unable to maintain himself or herself. The standard and the pleading differ across them.
2. Confirm the respondent has sufficient means, with particulars — this is not presumed, and neglect or refusal must be proved against it.
3. If urgency exists, do not wait for the main application to conclude — plead interim maintenance and litigation expenses under the second proviso to section 144(1) separately and expressly; the third proviso targets disposal of that interim application within sixty days of service of notice (a directory, not absolute, target).
4. If the applicant is a wife, check for the three section 144(4) disqualifications in advance — adultery, unreasonable refusal to live with the husband, or mutual-consent separation — and, where the husband's own bigamy or keeping a mistress is the real cause of estrangement, plead the Explanation to section 144(3)'s second proviso affirmatively to pre-empt the defence.
5. Choose the district deliberately — section 145(1) gives four venue options (where the respondent is; where he or the wife resides; where he last resided with the wife or the child's mother; where his parent resides), a genuinely wide choice favouring the dependant.
:::

```
[NAME OF APPLICANT]
[Wife / Child, through next friend / Father / Mother] of [NAME OF
RESPONDENT], aged about [AGE] years,
R/o [ADDRESS]                                                 ... APPLICANT

VERSUS

[NAME OF RESPONDENT]
R/o [ADDRESS]                                                ... RESPONDENT

APPLICATION FOR MAINTENANCE / INTERIM MAINTENANCE UNDER SECTION 144 OF THE
BHARATIYA NAGARIK SURAKSHA SANHITA, 2023

MOST RESPECTFULLY SHOWETH:

1.  That the applicant is the [wife / child / father / mother] of the
    respondent, and falls within clause [(a) / (b) / (c) / (d)] of
    section 144(1) of the BNSS, being unable to maintain [herself / himself
    / itself], for the following reasons: [STATE FACTS ESTABLISHING
    INABILITY TO MAINTAIN — for a child under clause (c), specifically
    plead the physical or mental abnormality or injury relied on].

2.  That this Hon'ble Court has jurisdiction under section 145(1), the
    [respondent resides / applicant resides / parties last resided
    together / applicant's parent resides] at [PLACE], within the local
    limits of this Court.

3.  That the respondent has sufficient means to maintain the applicant,
    in that [STATE PARTICULARS — income, property, occupation], and has
    neglected or refused to do so since on or about [DATE], as follows:
    [STATE FACTS OF NEGLECT OR REFUSAL].

4.  That the applicant is accordingly entitled to a monthly allowance for
    maintenance at the rate of Rs. [AMOUNT], having regard to the
    respondent's means and the applicant's reasonable needs.

5.  [Where interim relief is sought:] That the present application, if
    contested, is likely to take time to finally decide, and the
    applicant is in immediate need of support in the meantime. The
    applicant accordingly prays for interim maintenance and expenses of
    the proceeding under the second proviso to section 144(1), pending
    final disposal.

6.  [For a wife applicant, where relevant:] That the applicant is not
    disentitled under section 144(4), in that she is not living in
    adultery, has not refused without sufficient reason to live with the
    respondent, and the parties are not living separately by mutual
    consent. [Where applicable: That the respondent has contracted
    marriage with another woman / keeps a mistress, which is deemed just
    ground for the applicant's refusal to live with him, per the
    Explanation to the second proviso of section 144(3).]

7.  That this application is bona fide and made in genuine need.

PRAYER

It is, therefore, most respectfully prayed that this Hon'ble Court may be
pleased to:

(a) order the respondent to pay a monthly allowance of Rs. [AMOUNT] for
    the maintenance of the applicant, with effect from the date of this
    application;

(b) pending final disposal, order the respondent to pay interim
    maintenance of Rs. [AMOUNT] per month and litigation expenses of
    Rs. [AMOUNT], under the second proviso to section 144(1); and

(c) pass such other order(s) as this Hon'ble Court may deem fit and proper
    in the interest of justice.

AND for this act of kindness, the applicant shall, as duty bound, ever
pray.

[PLACE]
[DATE]
                                                          APPLICANT
                                                          Through Counsel
                                                          [ADVOCATE NAME]
                                                          [ENROLMENT NO.]
```

## Drafting pitfalls

::: warning Before you file
1. Do not plead the application generically across all four section 144(1) categories — identify precisely which one applies and plead the facts establishing "unable to maintain" for that category specifically; the standard is not identical across a wife, a minor child, an incapacitated adult child, and a parent.
2. Do not omit the interim-maintenance prayer where urgency is genuine. A bare final-relief application forces the dependant to wait out the entire main proceeding without support — plead interim maintenance and expenses expressly and separately under the second proviso to section 144(1).
3. Pray for maintenance from the date of the application, not the date of the order, at the outset — do not leave the start date to be fixed later without having asked for it.
4. For a wife respondent facing a section 144(4) disqualification argument built on her refusal to live with the husband, always check whether the Explanation to section 144(3)'s second proviso (bigamy or keeping a mistress) supplies a complete answer before conceding the point.
5. If the interim application sits undecided well past the sixty-day directory target under the third proviso to section 144(1), use the delay as leverage to press for expedited disposal — it is not, by itself, a self-executing entitlement to relief.
:::

::: danger The threshold trap
Section 144 is a welfare remedy, not an automatic entitlement — "sufficient means" and "neglect or refusal" must actually be proved, and a wife applicant's entitlement can be defeated outright under section 144(4) by proof of adultery, unreasonable refusal to live with the husband, or mutual-consent separation. An application that assumes entitlement without addressing these disqualifications in advance risks a refusal, or a later cancellation under section 144(5), that never reaches the question of the respondent's means at all.
:::

## Timing

Section 144(3)'s first proviso bars a warrant for levying any particular unpaid amount unless the application to levy it is made within one year from the date that amount became due — a rolling, per-default limitation, not a single limitation running from the original order. The third proviso to section 144(1) sets a sixty-day directory disposal target on interim-maintenance applications, running from service of notice. Where an order is made ex parte under section 145(2)'s proviso, the person against whom it is made has three months from the date of the order to apply to set it aside for good cause shown. If circumstances later change, or a competent Civil Court's decision becomes inconsistent with the order, raise a section 146 alteration or variation application promptly — the Magistrate's obligation under section 146(2) is not discretionary once that condition is met.
